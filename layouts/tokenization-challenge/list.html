01

The challenge

Overview

Tokenization is the first step in how language models turn human language into something they can process. That makes tokenizer design a fundamental part of building a language model.

But not every tokenizer represents every language equally efficiently. In this challenge, you will build one tokenizer for six languages under a fixed vocabulary budget of 10,000 tokens.

Your goal is simple

Represent text using as few tokens as possible, while avoiding unknown tokens.

Any tokenizer design is welcome. This is an open-ended, beginner-friendly competition built for learning through experimentation.

Team formation

You may participate individually or form a team. If you enter as a team, it must include participants from at least two different nationalities.

04

Starting points

Baselines

These two intentionally simple baselines show the central trade-off: shorter representations versus broader coverage.

Baseline 01

Word-level tokenizer

Each known word is represented by a single token. Words outside the vocabulary are represented using <UNK>.

This gives very short representations for known words, but it struggles when it encounters words it has never seen before.

Baseline 02

Character-level tokenizer

Text is represented using individual characters.

This provides much better coverage, but often requires many more tokens to represent the same text.

These baselines represent two different approaches to tokenization. Your job is to find something better.

What might you explore?

  • BPE
  • WordPiece
  • Unigram
  • Byte-level
  • Vocabulary allocation
  • Normalization
  • Pre-tokenization
  • Hybrid approaches

Try these, combine them, or develop your own idea. The challenge is intentionally open-ended.

How scoring works

Evaluation

Your tokenizer is evaluated on two things: how many tokens it needs to represent text, and how often it produces an unknown token.

Metric 01

Token fertility

Fl=number of tokens producednumber of words

Token fertility tells us how many tokens your tokenizer needs, on average, to represent one word.

150 tokens÷100 words=1.5

Lower fertility is better.

Metric 02

Unknown token rate

Ul=number of <UNK> tokensnumber of words

A tokenizer should ideally represent text without falling back to <UNK>. Unknown tokens are therefore heavily penalized.

Per-language result

Language score

Sl = fertilityFl + unknown-token penalty100 Ul

The multiplier of 100 makes unknown tokens expensive. Even a small unknown-token rate can noticeably increase your score.

From idea to entry

How to submit

The competition runs through GitHub. The process is designed to help you practise a real open-source workflow, even if this is your first Pull Request.

Rules and constraints

  • One tokenizer must support all six languages.
  • Maximum vocabulary size: 10,000 tokens.
  • Train using only the provided competition training data.
  • Any tokenizer approach is allowed.
  • The tokenizer must work without participant-written code during evaluation.
  • Your submission must be compatible with the official evaluation script.
  • The hidden test set determines leaderboard performance.

Good to know

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a GPU?

No. Tokenizer training and evaluation should be possible on a normal laptop or a free Colab environment.

Can I use any tokenizer algorithm?

Yes. Word-level, character-level, BPE, WordPiece, Unigram, byte-level, hybrids, or your own approach are all allowed.

Can I use more than 10,000 tokens?

No. The maximum vocabulary size is 10,000 tokens.

What happens if my tokenizer produces <UNK>?

Your submission is still valid, but unknown tokens receive a large penalty in the evaluation score.

Can I train on external datasets?

No. Use only the provided competition training data.

Do I need to train a language model?

No. You are only building the tokenizer.

What should I submit?

Your exported tokenizer in the format required by the starter kit.
